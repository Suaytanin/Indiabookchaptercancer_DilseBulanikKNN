A Linguistic Fuzzy Logic-Based KNN Framework for Breast Cancer Diagnosis with a GUI-Supported Clinical Decision Prototype
Hasan Suay Tanın1, Davut Kesen1, Memet Şahin1

1Department of Mathematics, Faculty of Science, Gaziantep University, Gaziantep, Türkiye
Corresponding author: Hasan Suay Tanın, hasansuaytanin@gmail.com. ORCID: Tanın 0009-0006-1330-5577; Kesen 0000-0002-2096-432X; Şahin 0000-0002-1066-1641.
Abstract Diagnostic decision support is most useful when it reports not only a class label but also how strong the evidence is. This chapter adds a linguistic fuzzy layer to the K-nearest neighbor (KNN) classifier: the KNN probability of malignancy is mapped by three triangular membership functions to the terms Low, Medium and High risk and aggregated into a single risk index R, which is read as Benign, Suspicious or Malignant. The framework is implemented as a Python/Tkinter prototype with live visualization of the membership functions and is evaluated on the Wisconsin Diagnostic Breast Cancer (WDBC) dataset. With K = 5 and a decision threshold of 0.40, the test-split accuracy is 94.74% and the malignant recall 90.48% (95.87% and 94.95% in repeated cross-validation). We show analytically that the Malignant decision of the fuzzy layer coincides exactly with the crisp 0.40 threshold, so the layer contributes a linguistic description rather than a different decision, and that the Suspicious category occupies 0.271 ≤ P < 0.40, which is empty at K = 5 but populated at finer posterior resolution. Lowering the Medium weight below 0.50 also makes it reachable at K = 5 and separates a confident Malignant group (100% precision on the test split) from an uncertain one. Logistic regression and an SVM reach higher accuracy than the KNN-based model, so the layer is positioned as a transparent reporting device rather than a more accurate classifier. A worked example and the complete fuzzy-layer code are included.
Keywords Fuzzy logic, Linguistic variables, Triangular membership function, Breast cancer diagnosis, K-nearest neighbor, Clinical decision support, Interpretability
